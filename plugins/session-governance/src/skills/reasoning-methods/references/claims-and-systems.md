# Claims and systems

## Claim-evidence

Type the claim before evaluating it: descriptive, causal, predictive, comparative, or normative. Bound its population, conditions, time, and as-of date. Seek both the strongest support and credible disconfirming evidence. Keep competing explanations alive only when evidence makes them plausible; do not create false balance. End with the claim's evidence boundary and update condition.

## System

Name the system boundary and time horizon. Trace current state or stocks, flows and transitions, incentives, reinforcing and balancing feedback loops, delays, and second-order effects. Check whether an apparent event is actually a delayed response or local optimization. State what was left outside the boundary.

## Divergence and convergence

During divergence, vary mechanisms rather than labels or colors and preserve why a route was abandoned. During convergence, compare routes against the same job, constraints, evidence, and reversible test. Do not average incompatible directions or decide by model voting.
