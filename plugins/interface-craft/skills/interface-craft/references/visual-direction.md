# Visual direction

Use this reference for a new surface or a material redesign, after inspecting the product's existing brand evidence.

1. Name the audience, their immediate job, and the one action or fact that deserves first attention.
2. Choose a hierarchy, density, typographic voice, color role, and spatial rhythm that support that job. Describe each choice in observable terms rather than labels such as “premium” or “modern.”
3. Identify the brand evidence being preserved: logo treatment, type, palette, imagery, vocabulary, or product conventions. If evidence conflicts, call out the conflict instead of inventing a blended style.
4. Commit to one coherent direction. Use contrast, scale, alignment, whitespace, and content order before adding decoration.
5. Check the direction against one plausible alternative and keep the option that better serves comprehension and action.

Avoid generic dashboard card grids, gratuitous gradients, decorative labels, and trend imitation that has no causal link to the product's job.
