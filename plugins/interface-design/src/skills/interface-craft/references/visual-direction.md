# Visual direction

Use this reference for a new surface or a material redesign, after inspecting the product's existing brand evidence.

1. Name the audience, their immediate job, and the one action or fact that deserves first attention. State a compact Design Read with the surface, audience, visual language, layout variance, motion intensity, and information density.
2. Freeze the communication core: product promise, intended audience outcome, exact one-sentence retell target, one signature cue, why that cue causally expresses the promise, its component/state/surface anchor, invariants, and prohibited drift. A visual motif without a semantic reason is decoration, not a signature cue.
3. Choose a hierarchy, density, typographic voice, color role, and spatial rhythm that support that job and make the signature cue legible. Describe each choice in observable terms rather than labels such as “premium” or “modern.”
4. Identify the brand evidence being preserved: logo treatment, type, palette, imagery, vocabulary, or product conventions. If evidence conflicts, call out the conflict instead of inventing a blended style.
5. Commit to one coherent direction. Use contrast, scale, alignment, whitespace, and content order before adding decoration.
6. Check the direction against one plausible alternative and keep the option that better serves comprehension, action, and the intended retell.

## Bounded direction exploration

Use this only when a material redesign has three genuinely plausible outcomes. Present exactly three named directions, each with a distinct axis position in layout, density, visual language, or interaction model. For each, give the observable decisions, when it wins, and its cost. Differences in accent color alone do not make separate directions. Ask one question to select the direction, then record the choice through [Design memory](design-memory.md). Do not build a picker or variants for routine extensions.

Avoid generic dashboard card grids, gratuitous gradients, decorative labels, and trend imitation that has no causal link to the product's job.
