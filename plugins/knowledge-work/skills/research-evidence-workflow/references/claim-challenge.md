# Claim challenge

Use this before discovery and again before seal. It sharpens claims without changing the research schema.

## Before discovery

For each decision-relevant statement, record in the brief or working notes:

- the smallest useful claim and its claim type: descriptive, causal, predictive, comparative, or normative;
- scope, population, conditions, and as-of date;
- a base rate only when a defensible reference class exists, including why it is comparable;
- the strongest credible competing explanation or alternative claim;
- an update condition: evidence or threshold that would change, narrow, or withdraw the claim.

Do not invent a numeric prior, force a base rate onto a unique event, or create false balance from implausible alternatives.

## Before seal

Search deliberately for disconfirming evidence and check whether apparently independent sources share the same upstream source. For every final claim, make the evidence boundary legible through the existing `basis`, `caveat`, and `limitation` fields. Put the update condition in the most appropriate of those existing fields. Do not add a confidence, probability, or challenge field to the claim schema.

An absent counterexample is not proof. A negative search supports only the bounded statement that the specified search did not find one.
