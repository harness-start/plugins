# Composition families

## Single symbol

Use one subject-specific object or shape. Declare its bbox, the intended viewing distance, and a void range rather than saying only “minimal”. A symbol that could be swapped into an unrelated subject is too generic.

## Figure-ground

Use one positive form and one negative-space reading. The second reading must remain discoverable at the review thumbnail size. Reject it when the hidden form needs explanatory copy to become visible.

## Scale contrast

Use a small subject against one large mass to express isolation, awe, pressure, or distance. State which emotion the scale relation serves and where supporting type remains readable.

## Type architecture

Display type can be a frame, path, mask, window, or structural interruption. Declare exact copy, type class, stroke profile, spacing rhythm, edge behavior, and whether each major type layer is in front of, behind, masking, or interrupted by imagery.

## Layered atmosphere

Use one primary subject with at most two supporting depth layers. Texture must not become an additional subject. Keep copy outside noisy regions or establish a measured contrast surface.

## Review questions

- Does one relationship clearly dominate?
- Does the declared void range survive the rendered result?
- Does exact copy remain readable at full size and thumbnail size?
- Is every texture or interruption doing semantic work?
- Would removing the subject name leave a subject-specific visual mechanism?
